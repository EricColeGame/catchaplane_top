## Weight vs. Speed: The Trade-Off That Defines Every Grab

Every aircraft sits somewhere on a spectrum between "easy but worthless" and "valuable but nearly impossible." Understanding that spectrum is the single most useful skill in the game, because it tells you when to commit and when to let a plane go.

| Weight class | Runway speed | Time inside grab range | Grip drain | Best suited glove tier |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Light | Slow | Long | Minimal | Starter gloves |
| Medium | Moderate | Medium | Noticeable | First or second upgrade |

A useful rule of thumb from player experience: if you fail a grab three times in a row on the same aircraft, you're not unlucky — you're under-geared. Go back to a lighter band, farm strength, and return. The runway isn't going anywhere.

### Why speed matters as much as weight

Speed controls your window. A slow plane gives you a generous arc to jump into, which is why light aircraft are the best teaching tool in the game. A fast plane compresses that window into a fraction of a second, which is where glove grab speed becomes the difference between a successful latch and a faceplant. This is exactly why the upgrade path in the game pairs "faster" and "heavier" together — they're two halves of the same problem.

Gear boxes add a third lever. Jetpacks, parachutes, and Gravity Wings won't grab a plane for you, but they can save a bad hold, extend a fall, or let you reposition for a second attempt. Community reports suggest saving gear for the heavy-plane phase rather than burning it early on light aircraft you can already catch reliably.

## FAQ

**How many Catch a Plane plane types are there?**

The game doesn't publish an official count, and the roster grows with updates. In practice, players sort aircraft into roughly five bands based on weight and speed — light props, regional jets, narrow-body airliners, heavy cargo and widebody jets, and rare showpiece aircraft. Treat those bands as a practical map rather than a fixed list.

**Which Catch a Plane plane types should I target first?**

Start with the lightest aircraft on the runway. They're slow, forgiving, and ideal for learning jump timing and grab arcs. Move to regional jets once your grabs are reliable, then push into narrow-body airliners as your gloves improve.

**Why can't I hold onto heavy planes even with good gloves?**

Gloves control whether you can latch; strength controls how long you keep the latch. If heavy planes slip through your fingers, you likely need strength training alongside glove upgrades — and a parachute or Gravity Wing as a safety net for the attempt.

**Do gear boxes affect which plane types I can catch?**

Indirectly, yes. Jetpacks, parachutes, and Gravity Wings don't change the weight requirement, but they can rescue a failing hold or give you a second attempt. Community reports suggest saving gear for the heavy-plane phase, where a single save can turn a failed run into a trophy.
