## How Grabbing Actually Works: Weight, Speed, and Grip

Every plane you chase is really two stats in a trench coat: how fast it's moving and how much it weighs. Faster planes pass you sooner, giving a smaller window to time your jump. Heavier planes drain grip more aggressively once you're attached. Your gloves set the ceiling on what you can grab at all, and your trained strength sets how long you can keep holding.

That creates a triangle you're constantly balancing:

- **Grab speed** — how quickly your gloves latch onto a passing plane
- **Weight limit** — the heaviest aircraft your gear allows you to catch
- **Grip endurance** — how many seconds you survive the drag

If any one of those is underleveled, the other two stop mattering. Community reports consistently describe players stalling out because they chased heavy planes with starter gloves, or trained strength for hours while still grabbing the slowest aircraft on the runway.

Start with the slowest, lightest aircraft on the runway. They give you the widest jump window and the gentlest grip drain, which is exactly what you need to learn timing before worrying about weight limits or heavy planes.

No. Progression comes from grip time and upgrades, not from a single dramatic grab. A consistent run on mid-tier planes with upgraded gloves will outperform risky attempts on aircraft your build can't hold.

Treat gloves as your main progression track rather than an occasional purchase. If you've failed the same plane type three times in a row, that's your signal — the next upgrade is due.
