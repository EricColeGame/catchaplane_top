## Reading the Runway: How Rare Planes Announce Themselves

The single biggest mistake newer players make is committing to the first plane that comes within reach. Rare aircraft usually telegraph themselves a second or two before they arrive, and that window is everything.

| Signal | What it usually means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Deep, low engine tone | A heavier aircraft is on approach | Stop drifting; plant yourself on the centerline |
| Larger silhouette at distance | More mass, more grip required | Check your glove tier before you jump |
| Faster approach speed | Smaller timing window | Jump early rather than late |
| High pass altitude | Harder grab angle | Reposition upslope or use a movement tool |
| Long gap between spawns | Spawn cluster may be building | Hold position, do not burn stamina |

Players generally sort traffic into rough bands. The exact names vary by community, but the behavior does not:

| Band (community shorthand) | Relative speed | Grip drain | What it demands | Best use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Starter airliners | Slow | Low | Almost nothing | Warming up, testing a new glove |
| Regional jets | Medium | Medium | Decent glove tier | Reliable trophy income |
| Wide-body heavies | Fast | High | Upgraded gloves + trained strength | Big jumps in progress |
| Rare and event spawns | Fastest | Highest | Everything you have | World unlocks, record runs |

Notice that the bands are really a ladder of *requirements*, not just rewards. A wide-body you cannot hold is worse than a regional jet you can — you burn the attempt, lose the position, and wait for the next spawn cycle. Match the plane to your gear, not to your ambition.

## Gloves, Grip, and Gear: Preparing for Catch a Plane Rare Planes

Catch a Plane gives you three levers to pull before you ever leave the ground: strength training, glove upgrades, and gear boxes. Each one solves a different failure mode.

| Upgrade path | What it fixes | Priority for rare hunting |
|---|---|---|
| Strength training | How long you can hold on once attached | High — it is the floor under every run |
| Glove tier | Grab speed and how heavy a plane you can catch | Highest — without it, rares simply refuse to stick |
| Gear boxes | Jetpacks, parachutes, Gravity Wings | Medium — situational, but run-saving |

The logic is straightforward. Strength decides how long a hold lasts, so it sets your ceiling on every plane you touch. Gloves decide *whether the grab connects at all* and whether a heavy aircraft is even an option, which makes glove tier the real gate on rare-plane hunting. A player with great strength and starter gloves will simply watch the good traffic fly past.

Gear boxes fill the gaps those two cannot. Each tool answers a specific "I almost had it" moment:

| Gear item | What it does for you | Best moment to use |
|---|---|---|
| Jetpack | Extends airtime and recovers a botched approach | Right after a missed grab on a fast plane |
| Parachute | Softens the end of a long hold | Late in a run when you are far from the runway |
| Gravity Wings | Adds mid-air control | When a rare pass comes in at a bad angle |

Do not hoard boxes for a hypothetical perfect run. Open them, learn what each tool feels like, and you will recognize the moment a jetpack or a set of Gravity Wings turns a lost cause into a completed hold. That instinct is worth more than any single item.
